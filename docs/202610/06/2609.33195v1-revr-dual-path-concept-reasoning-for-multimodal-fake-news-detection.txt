REVR: DUAL-PATH CONCEPT REASONING FOR MULTIMODAL FAKE NEWS
DETECTION
Zhikai Tan⋆, Yuzhou Yang⋆, Qichao Ying⋆, Pinjie Xu†, Sheng Li⋆, Zhenxing Qian⋆and Xinpeng Zhang⋆
⋆Fudan University, † China University of Mining and Technology - Beijing
ABSTRACT
Vision-language models (VLMs) support multimodal fake
news detection (FND) by producing explicit analyses. Re-
cent methods further improve interpretability by organizing
verification knowledge into explicit concepts. However, two
questions remain: how to improve the reliability and applica-
bility of verification concepts, and how to effectively apply
reusable concepts to verify unseen news. We propose ReVR,
a dual-path reasoning framework that constructs and applies
reusable verification concepts for multimodal fake news de-
tection. An agentic workflow grounds and consolidates can-
didate concepts, while statistical profiles characterize their
historical behavior.
During inference, a coverage-oriented
path aggregates evidence from the complete concept library
using a trainable encoder, while a query-focused path prompts
a frozen VLM to reason over selected concepts and their
observations.
A learned conflict resolver selects between
the two predictions when they disagree.
Experiments on
fake news benchmarks demonstrate the effectiveness of the
method regarding detection performance and generalizability.
Index Terms— Multimodal Fake News Detection, Vision-
Language Models, Concept Bottlenecks
1. INTRODUCTION
Multimodal fake news detection aims to determine news ve-
racity by jointly analyzing textual and visual content. The
task involves assessing both the authenticity of each modality
and the consistency of their content.
Existing approaches
learn multimodal representations through text-image fu-
sion [1, 2] and model cross-modal consistency or ambigu-
ity [3]. More fine-grained analysis examines image and text
manipulation cues [4], local cross-modal correlations [5, 6],
and alignment of entities, events, and temporal informa-
tion [7]. However, individual verification cues can vary in
relevance and reliability across news items, and inaccurate
intermediate assessments may affect the final prediction.
This motivates characterizing their behavior across samples
and studying how to apply them effectively to unseen news.
Although news items differ in topic and presentation, ver-
ification often relies on recurring questions about text-image
consistency, entity agreement, and factual support. Thus, be-
yond producing free-form rationales, a growing line of work
converts recurring verification cues into explicit intermedi-
ate knowledge that can be inspected by human and reused
by models [8]. These studies extend feature-based predic-
tion toward explicit reasoning over human-interpretable con-
cepts and their relations, building on concept bottlenecks [9]
and learned logical rules [10]. TELLER [11] uses large lan-
guage models to evaluate expert-defined fact-checking pred-
icates and learns interpretable logical rules. MiRAGe [12]
combines object-level visual concepts and automatically dis-
covered textual concepts with embedding-based classifiers.
PCGR [13] discovers interrogative concepts from high-loss
multimodal samples, organizes them into an evolving layered
graph, and performs hierarchical probabilistic reasoning.
Despite improved interpretability and performance of
the literature works, two questions remain: 1) How can we
improve the reliability and applicability of proposed verifica-
tion concepts? Both predefined predicates and automatically
discovered concepts may be biased or limited in scope, and
VLM-generated analyses may contain hallucinations.
As
simple filtering, such as deduplication, may leave these issues
unresolved, grounding concepts in source evidence and build-
ing historical statistical profiles are important for assessing
their reliability and applicability. 2) How can we further im-
prove the effectiveness of applying reusable concepts to verify
unseen news? Assessing many concepts may include weakly
relevant cues, whereas detailed reasoning over a selected
subset may overlook complementary evidence. So beyond
modeling concept dependencies, effective verification must
balance broad assessment with contextual reasoning,
We propose ReVR, a dual-path reasoning framework that
constructs and applies reusable verification concepts for mul-
timodal fake news detection. We first build a reusable con-
cept library by prompting a frozen VLM to propose candi-
dates from training news, ground their analyses in source evi-
dence, and consolidate related candidates into canonical con-
cepts. We check each canonical concept against its associated
samples and summarize its historical behavior in a statistical
profile. For unseen news, the query-focused path prompts the
same frozen VLM to reason over selected concepts, while the
coverage-oriented path uses a trainable encoder and classi-
fier to aggregate evidence from the full library with historical
profiles. A trainable conflict resolver retains the paths’ com-

2. PROPOSED METHOD
Figure 1 presents the two stages of ReVR, i.e., the Concept
Proposal and Consolidation stage, and the Dual-path Concept
Verification stage.
2.1. Concept Proposal and Consolidation
Given training news items x = (T, I) with text T, image I,
and veracity label y, we generate and ground raw candidate
concepts ˜c. We consolidate related candidates into M canoni-
cal concepts {cm}M
m=1 and profile their historical behavior to
obtain a reusable concept library C.
Candidate Concept Proposal & Grounding. A lightweight
agentic workflow prompts a frozen VLM to construct con-
cepts in three steps. 1) Thinking: For each training item x,
it proposes distinct raw candidate concepts ˜c. Each candidate
contains a stance-neutral verification angle, a sample-specific
analysis, and an assessment of whether the evidence favors
real news, fake news, or neither. 2) Inspecting: It invokes
local tools as needed, including text-span inspection, OCR,
and image cropping, to locate supporting text spans or image
regions. The thinking and inspecting steps repeat until the
VLM emits an exit status. 3) Grounding: The VLM checks
whether the located evidence supports the analysis and is rel-
evant to the proposed angle, and it can directly reject candi-
dates that it claims unsupported. The retained candidates’ se-
mantic content is used for consolidation, while their real/fake
assessments are retained for profiling.
Concept Consolidation & Assessment. To construct reusable
verification angles with consistent cross-instance applicabil-
ity, ReVR consolidates the generated candidates in two it-
erative stages. 1) Grouping: The VLM groups semantically
similar retained candidates and removes duplicates.
Each
group is represented by a canonical concept cm. 2) Assessing
and refining: The VLM checks each canonical concept cm
against its group members, and refines and rechecks con-
cepts with member recall below an empirical threshold of
0.7. Accordingly, concepts whose cluster center fails the re-
call requirement after three modifications are released again
to the candidate pool and we redo the consolidation. After
iterative consolidation and assessment, we let VLM assign
each retained group a concise canonical name and verification
scope for cm, and the retained canonical concepts are then
used for profiling and inference.
Concept Profiling. For each canonical concept cm, we pro-
file three complementary historical properties. Specifically,
πm = [bm, ρm, um].
1) Authenticity tendency bm is the
average signed direction, with positive values favoring fake
news and negative values favoring real news. 2) Label agree-
ment ρm, the fraction of applicable assessments matching the
ground-truth labels, provides a reliability prior. 3) Ambiguity
um, measured as the frequency of non-directional assess-
ments, enables uncertainty-aware weighting. The complete
library is C = {(cm, πm)}M
m=1 , where M is the number of
retained canonical concepts and πm is the historical profile
of cm. Profiles are computed from training data and fixed
during testing.
2.2. Dual-path Concept Verification
Given an unseen news item x and library C, ReVR obtains
predictions through query-focused (a) reasoning over selected
concepts and coverage-oriented (g) evidence encoding across
the full library.
We retain their common verdict or select
between disagreeing predictions using a learned conflict re-


solver, and return the final prediction by with a corresponding
rationale for interpretation.
Query-Focused Concept Reasoning. To capture instance-
specific evidence, the frozen VLM performs three steps. 1)
Retrieving: A retrieval prompt sequentially selects k distinct
concepts from C that are most relevant to x. 2) Observing: An
observation prompt examines the news text and image under
each selected concept’s verification scope. And 3) Aggregat-
ing: An aggregation prompt jointly reasons over the resulting
concept-observation pairs to produce a natural-language ra-
tionale and prediction bya. This path is fully prompt-driven
without tunable parameters.
Coverage-Oriented Concept Reasoning.
To get comple-
mentary evidences over all discovered concepts, the coverage-
oriented path assesses x under every concept in C, preserving
evidence for both veracity hypotheses [14,15]:

3. EXPERIMENTS AND RESULTS
Experimental Setups. All VLM calls use Qwen3.5-Plus as
backend [18] For each training instance, we generate three
candidate concepts and encode them with frozen mpnet-v2 [21,
22]. We apply average-linkage agglomerative clustering over
cosine distances with threshold δ = 0.25 and retain clusters
with no less than an empirical ten instances for each. The en-
coder uses a 768-to-32 semantic projection and a two-layer,
four-head Transformer and is trained with AdamW optimizer
with learning rate 10−3, batch size 64, and 100 epochs. The
query-focused path sequentially retrieves k = 3 non-repeated
concepts. As for datasets, following the evaluation protocol
of many baselines [13], we evaluate on MMFakeBench [23],
AMG [7], and MiRAGeNews [12].

We train each method on
one source dataset and test it on the other two without target-
dataset adaptation. ReVR ranks first in every direction and
raises average Macro-F1 from 49.0 to 68.5, showing that
we can successfully mine reusable verification concepts with
generalizability beyond their source dataset.

4. CONCLUSION
We propose ReVR, which consolidates VLM-generated anal-
yses into reusable verification concepts.
Complementary
query-focused and coverage-oriented paths apply these con-
cepts to unseen news, with a learned conflict resolver select-
ing between their predictions when they disagree. Extensive
experiments validate the effectiveness of the method.
